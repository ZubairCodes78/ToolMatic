How to Compress Images for Your Website to Supercharge Page Speed

Uncompressed images account for over 60% of total web page weight. Learn how to optimize hero banners, e-commerce products, and blog visuals for peak SEO performance.

In Short

To pass Google Core Web Vitals and achieve page load speeds under 2 seconds, compress full-width hero images to under 150 KB (max 1920px width in WebP format), keep blog body pictures under 70 KB (max 1200px width), and size thumbnails under 30 KB. Always declare width and height HTML attributes to prevent CLS layout shifts.

Why Image Optimization is Critical for Google Rankings

Google officially incorporates page experience metrics — known as Core Web Vitals — into its search ranking algorithms. The most critical metric, Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), measures how long it takes for the largest visual element on the screen (frequently a hero image or banner) to finish rendering. Pages that take longer than 2.5 seconds to load suffer lower rankings and higher bounce rates.

Best Practices for Web Image SEO
  • Always include descriptive alt text containing natural keywords.
  • Add explicit width and height attributes to prevent Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS).
  • Use loading="lazy" on all below-the-fold images.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum recommended image size for web pages?

+

For standard blog and content pages, individual body images should not exceed 100 KB, and full-screen hero banners should stay under 150 KB. The total weight of all assets on a single page should ideally stay below 1.5 MB.
